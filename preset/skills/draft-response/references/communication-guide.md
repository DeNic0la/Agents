# Customer communication guide

## Principles

1. **Empathy first:** acknowledge their situation before solutions.
2. **Direct:** bottom line up front. Customers are busy.
3. **Honest:** never overpromise, mislead or hide bad news in jargon.
4. **Specific:** concrete details, dates and names.
5. **Own it:** "we", not "the system" or "the process".
6. **Close the loop:** every response has a clear next step.
7. **Match their energy:** empathy for frustration, enthusiasm for excitement.

## Structure

1. **Acknowledge** (1-2 sentences): what they said or are experiencing.
2. **Core message** (1-3 paragraphs): the answer or update, concrete.
3. **Next steps** (1-3 bullets): what you'll do and by when, what they need to do, when they'll hear from you.
4. **Close** (1 sentence): warm, professional, available.

## Length

- Chat: 1-4 sentences.
- Ticket reply: 1-3 short paragraphs, scannable.
- Email: at most 3-5 paragraphs.
- Escalation reply: as long as needed, structured with headers.
- Executive: 2-3 paragraphs at most, data-driven.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone | Characteristics |
|-----------|------|-----------------|
| Good news | Celebratory | Warm, congratulatory, forward-looking |
| Routine update | Professional | Clear, concise, friendly |
| Technical answer | Precise | Accurate, structured, patient |
| Delay | Accountable | Honest, apologetic, specific, action-oriented |
| Bad news | Candid | Direct, empathetic, solution-oriented |
| Issue or outage | Urgent | Immediate, transparent, reassuring |
| Escalation | Executive | Composed, owning it, presenting a plan |
| Billing | Precise | Factual, empathetic, resolution-focused |

## Tone by relationship

- **New (0-3 months):** more formal, extra explanation, offer help and resources proactively, build trust through reliability.
- **Established:** warm and collaborative, reference shared history, more direct.
- **Frustrated or escalated:** extra acknowledgment, fast responses, concrete commitments, short feedback loops.

## Style

Do:

- Active voice: "We'll investigate", not "This will be investigated".
- "I" for personal commitments, "we" for team ones.
- Name who does what: "Sarah from engineering will...".
- Use the customer's terms, not internal jargon.
- Absolute dates: "by Friday, January 24", not "in a few days".
- Headers or bullets for long replies.

Don't:

- Buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage").
- Blame other teams, systems or processes.
- Passive voice to dodge ownership ("mistakes were made").
- Hedging that undermines confidence.
- CC people who don't need to be there.
- More than one exclamation mark per email.

## By situation

- **Product question:** answer first, link docs, offer the right contact. If you don't know: say so, commit to finding out, give a date.
- **Issue or bug:** acknowledge the impact, say what's known and the status, give a workaround, set expectations for resolution, commit to regular updates.
- **Escalation:** acknowledge severity and frustration, own it without excuses, give an action plan with a timeline and an accountable person, offer a call if the severity warrants it.
- **Bad news (sunset, delay, can't fix):** say it directly, explain the reasoning honestly, acknowledge their specific impact, offer alternatives, give a path forward.
- **Good news:** lead with the outcome, connect it to their goals, suggest next steps, be genuinely enthusiastic.
- **Declining a request (feature, discount, exception):** acknowledge the request and its reason, be honest about the decision and why, offer alternatives, leave the door open.

## Follow-up timing

| Situation | Follow up |
|-----------|-----------|
| Unanswered question | 2-3 business days |
| Open issue | Daily if critical, every 2-3 days if standard |
| After a meeting | Notes within 24 hours, then check at the deadline |
| Ongoing issue | As needed |
| After bad news | 1 week, to check impact and sentiment |

## When to escalate

**To your manager:** the customer threatens to cancel or downsell, asks for a policy exception you can't authorize, an issue is past its SLA, they ask for leadership contact, or you made an error that needs senior help.

**To product or engineering:** a critical bug blocks their business, a feature gap is losing a deal, unique technical requirements beyond standard support, integration problems needing engineering investigation.
