# Confidence, escalation and knowledge capture

## Confidence by source tier

| Tier | Source | Confidence | Notes |
|------|--------|------------|-------|
| 1 | Official docs, knowledge base, policies | High | Trust unless outdated; check dates |
| 2 | CRM, support tickets, meeting notes | Medium-high | May be subjective or incomplete |
| 3 | Chat, email, calendar notes | Medium | Informal, may lack context or speculate |
| 4 | Web, forums, third-party docs | Low-medium | May not match your situation |
| 5 | Inference, analogy, best practice | Low | Flag as inference, not fact |

## Confidence levels

Always state one.

- **High:** confirmed by an official or authoritative source, or several sources agree, and it's current. "I'm confident this is accurate, based on [source]."
- **Medium:** informal sources only, a single uncorroborated source, or slightly dated. "Based on [source] this appears to be the case; confirm with [team or person]."
- **Low:** inferred, outdated or contradictory sources. "I couldn't find a definitive answer. My best assessment is [answer], but verify it before sharing with the customer."
- **Unable to determine:** nothing relevant found, or it needs expertise you don't have. "I couldn't find information on this. Ask [expert or team]."

## Contradicting sources

1. Name the contradiction.
2. Find which source is more authoritative or more recent.
3. Present both with context.
4. Recommend how to resolve it.
5. For a customer, use the most conservative answer until resolved.

## Answer directly or escalate

**Answer directly when** official docs clearly cover it, reliable sources agree, it's factual and not sensitive, it involves no commitments, timelines or pricing, and similar answers proved accurate before.

**Escalate or verify when** it involves roadmap commitments or timelines; pricing, legal or contract terms; security, compliance or data handling; it could set a precedent or create expectations; sources contradict; it concerns a customer's custom configuration; it needs expertise you lack; or the customer is at risk and a wrong answer would make it worse.

**Escalate to:**

1. a subject matter expert for technical or domain questions
2. the product team for roadmap, feature or capability questions
3. legal or compliance for terms, privacy, security or regulation
4. billing or finance for pricing, invoices, payments
5. engineering for custom configurations, bugs, root causes
6. leadership for strategic decisions, exceptions, high stakes

## Writing it down for the team

Document when the question recurs, the research took real effort or combined several sources, it corrects a common misunderstanding, or it's easy to get wrong.

```
## [Question or topic]

**Last verified:** [date]
**Confidence:** [level]

### Answer
[Direct answer]

### Details
[Supporting detail and nuance]

### Sources
[Where it came from]

### Related questions
[Other questions it answers]

### Review notes
[When to re-verify; what could change the answer]
```

Date-stamp entries, flag ones tied to product versions, review quarterly, archive stale ones, tag by topic, product area and segment.
