# Competitive brief

Write a competitive analysis brief for one or more competitors, or for one feature area across competitors.

## 1. Scope the analysis

Ask the user:

- **Competitors:** which ones, or which feature area to compare across them?
- **Focus:** full product, one feature area, pricing and packaging, go-to-market, or positioning?
- **Decision:** what will this inform? Product strategy, sales enablement, board or investor material, feature prioritization.

## 2. Research

- **Web:** product and feature pages, pricing pages, changelogs and launch posts, press and analyst coverage, reviews (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius), job postings (they signal direction), community discussions.
- **Knowledge base, if one is connected:** existing competitive analyses, win/loss reports, battle cards.
- **Team chat, if connected:** competitor mentions in sales and product channels, recent deal feedback.

If a source isn't available, say so and work from what is.

## 3. Write the brief

Sections, in order:

1. **Competitor overview:** per competitor, company summary (founded, size, funding or revenue if public, target market), how they position themselves, recent momentum (launches, funding, partnerships, customer wins).
2. **Feature comparison:** a table by the capability areas buyers use, with a consistent rating scale.
3. **Positioning analysis:** target customer, category claim, key differentiator, value proposition.
4. **Strengths and weaknesses:** where they really excel and fall short, with evidence (what customers praise and complain about). Don't dismiss competitors or inflate their weaknesses.
5. **Opportunities:** gaps in their offerings, needs nobody serves well, bets of theirs we disagree with, market shifts that favor us.
6. **Threats:** where they invest heavily, moves that could disrupt us, where we're most vulnerable, the nightmare scenario.
7. **Strategic implications:** what to build, accelerate or deprioritize; where to differentiate vs. reach parity; positioning changes; what to monitor. Keep it short and actionable. This is where the value is.

Read [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md) when building the competitive set, the feature matrix or the positioning analysis, or when the user asks for win/loss analysis or market trends.

## 4. Follow up

Offer to go deeper on a section, write a one-page executive summary, draft battle cards or a "how to win against X" guide, or set up a monitoring plan.

## Rules

- Rate from real product experience, customer feedback and reviews, not marketing claims. A comparison where we always win isn't credible.
- Weight by what matters to target customers, not feature count. Customers don't care about architecture elegance.
- Pricing is hard to compare fairly. Note the caveats: different packaging, usage-based vs. seat-based, custom enterprise pricing.
- Reviews and job postings are underrated sources: unfiltered user opinion and strategic direction.
- Date the brief and flag the areas that change fast.
